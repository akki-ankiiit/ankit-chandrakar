Chapter 01

The idea

Neostra is a privacy operations and compliance platform. Privacy teams use it to map data, answer requests, manage consent and prove compliance, all in one place. The identity has one job: make that work feel calm, oriented and protected.

A new star

The name joins two words. Neo means new. Astra is Latin for the stars. Neostra is a new star: a fixed point to steer by when the rules keep moving and the data keeps growing.

Sailors trusted the North Star because it holds still while everything else turns around it. That is the role Neostra wants to play for a privacy team: the one reference point that stays true.

The space for privacy.

The tagline carries two meanings at once. Space is the night sky where the star lives. Space is also room: a protected place where personal data is kept safe and handled with care. The symbol draws both meanings. A star sits inside an open orbit that guards it without caging it.

Chapter 02

Where the mark came from

The symbol was not invented from nothing. It was found by simplifying a compass rose, one step at a time, until only the parts that carry meaning were left. These are the original working sketches.

1

The compass rose

The search began with the oldest tool for finding your way. A compass rose is calm, symmetric and trusted. It points, it orients, it never panics.

2

One needle, pulled out

The rose was reduced to its main needle. The needle was turned off true north onto a diagonal axis, shown in green, so the mark leans forward and feels in motion instead of static.

3

The star

The diagonal needle was crossed with a short arm at right angles. Long arms of about twice the short arms turned the needle into a four-point star, sharp at the tips and soft at the joins.

4

The orbit

Arcs were drawn around the star to give it a place to live. The dotted line shows the full circle the arcs imply. The orbit never closes completely, because privacy should protect people without locking them in.

5

The Neostra symbol

The two sketched arcs became two offset circles cut from one disc. The heavy crescent moved to the trailing side. The star breaks out of the orbit at both tips, as if it is moving forward through it.

Start with a tool for finding your way. Remove everything that does not help you find it. Give what remains a safe place to move.

The design principle behind every decision in this book.

Chapter 03

Anatomy of the symbol

The symbol has two parts that always travel together: the North Star and the orbit around it. Knowing the parts makes the rules in later chapters easier to follow.

  • 1  North Star

    The four-point star at the centre. It is the brand in one shape: a fixed point to steer by.

  • 2  Crescent

    The heavy side of the orbit. It sits on the trailing side and gives the mark weight, like a moon behind the star.

  • 3  Orbit

    The thin side of the ring. It finishes the circle lightly, so the mark stays open on the leading side.

  • 4  Breakout points

    The long arms pass through the orbit at both ends. The star is held, not trapped.

  • 5  Open space

    The two counters between star and orbit. This is the space in the tagline, kept clean at every size.

The orbit is shown in a pale tint here only to separate the parts. In the logo, star and orbit are always one colour.

Chapter 04

Construction

The symbol is built from three circles and two crossed axes. The numbers below come from the master artwork and are given so the mark can be checked, never redrawn. Always use the supplied files.

A orbit, outer edgeB inner edge of the crescentC inner edge of the orbit

Three circles

Circle A sets the outer edge of the orbit. Circles B and C are each 0.99 × the size of A and are pushed off-centre in opposite directions along one diagonal, each by about a quarter of A’s radius. Where B is cut away, the thick crescent remains on the lower left. Where C is cut away, the thin orbit remains on the upper right.

Two axes

The star sits on a cross of two axes at right angles, like the cardinal points of a compass. The whole cross is turned 31.7° anticlockwise from true north. That lean is fixed. It gives the star its sense of forward motion.

Chapter 05

The wordmark

NEOSTRĀ is set in classical serif capitals with wide, even spacing. The serif gives the name the authority of something written down and kept. The open spacing gives it air. The wordmark is drawn artwork, not live type.

Wide spacing

The space between letters, marked in green, is about 0.8 × the cap height. Optical spacing is used instead of mechanical, so the round O and the open Λ do not leave holes. Never close up or stretch the spacing.

The open A

The last letter is an A without its crossbar. It rises to a point, echoing the top arm of the North Star, and keeps the end of the word light. Do not replace it with a standard A.

The macron

The bar over the final letter marks the long ā of astra, the star in the name. It also reads as a horizon line with something rising above it. It is part of the wordmark and must never be removed.

Writing the name

In sentences, write Neostra with a capital N and no macron. Never write NEOSTRA in capitals in running text, and never type the macron in body copy. The capitals and the macron belong to the logo only.

Why it is artwork

Retyping the name in Crimson Pro or any other font will not reproduce the spacing, the open A or the macron. Always place the supplied wordmark file.

Chapter 06

Logo family

There are five approved versions. Choose by the shape of the space and how much room you have. When in doubt, use the primary stacked logo.

Primary stacked

The default logo. Use it wherever the space is roughly square or there is room to breathe: covers, home pages, packaging, signage.

Stacked with tagline

For brand moments where the promise matters: campaign end frames, event backdrops, the last slide of a pitch. Not for small sizes or product UI.

Horizontal

For wide, short spaces: website and product headers, email signatures, document footers.

Symbol

For square or tiny spaces where the brand is already known: app icon, favicon, social avatar, loading screens.

Wordmark

Rarely needed. Use only where the symbol already appears nearby, for example on the back of a business card.

Chapter 07

Clear space

Clear space keeps the logo from being crowded by text, edges and other marks. It is measured with x, the cap height of the wordmark, so it scales with the logo automatically.

Stacked logo. Keep at least 1x clear on every side. With the tagline, measure from the tagline’s baseline.

Horizontal logo. x is the cap height of its wordmark, so the clear space is smaller in absolute terms.

Symbol on its own. x is one third of the symbol’s height, which matches its proportion in the stacked logo.

Clear space is a minimum. Give the logo more room whenever you can, especially on covers and home pages, where 2x or more looks best.

Never let a photo edge, a line of text, a button or a partner logo enter the clear space. Partner logos sit at least 2x away and are divided by a 1 px rule in Border colour.

Contrast

Measured with the WCAG 2.2 formula. Neostra Green on white is 2.4 : 1. That is fine for the symbol, which is a large graphic, but too low for text. For green text on light backgrounds, use Deep Green.

White on Neostra Green fails for both text and the logo, so the logo on green is always Forest 950.

Two typefaces, each with one job. Crimson Pro is the voice: classical, literary, trustworthy. DM Sans is the instrument: clear, modern and built for interfaces. Together they echo the logo’s serif wordmark and its clean, geometric symbol.

Rules

  • Crimson Pro for headlines, DM Sans for everything a person reads to get something done.
  • Set headlines in sentence case. Never set text in all capitals; capitals belong to the wordmark.
  • Body text 16 px minimum on screen, 9 pt in print, with line height 1.5 to 1.6 and lines under 80 characters.
  • Tighten large Crimson Pro headlines slightly, around −1.5%. Leave DM Sans body text at default spacing.
  • Neither typeface is used to write the logo. Always place the wordmark artwork.
  • If the brand fonts are unavailable, for example in email or Office files, fall back to Georgia and Arial.

Chapter 12

The North Star

The star at the centre of the symbol can work on its own as a brand device. It is the brand’s pointer: it marks the one thing that matters most in a view, the current step, the key number, the recommended choice.

Because it is cut directly from the logo, it carries the brand without needing the name. Used sparingly, it becomes a signal people learn to trust. Used everywhere, it becomes decoration. The rules below keep it a signal.

  • One star per view

    There is only one North Star. Never repeat it as a pattern, a starfield or a row of icons.

  • Fixed lean

    The star always leans 31.7° anticlockwise, exactly as in the logo. Never rotate, flip or spin it.

  • Approved colours

    Neostra Green on Canvas, Surface, Forest and Ink. Forest 950 on Neostra Green. Mint 400 on Forest below 24 px.

  • Room around it

    Keep clear space of at least half the star’s width on every side.

  • Never in a ring

    Do not put the star inside a circle, badge or new orbit. The orbit belongs to the logo.

  • Not the logo

    The star alone does not replace the logo. The brand must be introduced first with a full logo or the symbol.

Chapter 14

Tagline

The space for privacy. Five words that say what Neostra gives a privacy team: room to work, and a protected place for the data in their care.

The tagline is locked to the stacked logo as artwork, set in DM Sans and centred under the wordmark. Use the supplied file. Do not reset it.

The space for privacy.

In headlines, the tagline may be set on its own in Crimson Pro Light, in sentence case with its full stop.

  • Always written “The space for privacy.” with a capital T and a closing full stop.
  • Never in capitals, never in quotation marks, never followed by an exclamation mark.
  • Do not translate it into other wording or shorten it. For other languages, agree a translation with the brand team.
  • Do not attach it to the horizontal logo or the symbol. If a layout needs both, set the tagline as a separate line at least 2x from the logo.
  • Use it once per piece. It is a sign-off, not a slogan to repeat.

Misuse

The logo only works if it looks the same everywhere. These are the mistakes seen most often. If a layout seems to need one of them, the layout needs to change, not the logo.

✕

Do not stretch or squash the logo.

✕

Do not rotate the logo.

✕

Do not change the angle of the star.

✕

Do not recolour the symbol.

✕

Do not use white on Neostra Green.

✕

Do not add shadows, glows or effects.

✕

Do not outline the logo.

✕

Do not rearrange the parts.

Neostra
✕

Do not retype the name.

✕

Do not change the proportions.

Also avoid placing the logo on busy photographs, patterns or gradients. If it must sit on an image, choose a calm, dark area and use the one-colour white version, or place the logo on a Forest 950 panel.

Never animate the parts of the logo separately except in approved brand films, and never redraw, trace or rebuild it from a screenshot. Use the master files listed in chapter 18.
